Central New York Weekend Guide

What to Do With the Kids: September 19–20

A mass ascension of hot-air balloons, an 80th-anniversary hometown festival, red-panda keeper talks and three more plans that are actually worth your family's time.

Saturday is the day to commit

As of Wednesday morning, Saturday looks cool but workable across the region: partly sunny to mostly cloudy, with highs around 61–67°F. Sunday carries a broader morning-rain risk from Syracuse through Palmyra, Inlet and Queensbury. Put the balloon festival, zoo or Adirondack drive on Saturday. Keep Sunday local and flexible, and recheck wind—not just rain—before driving for balloons or a chairlift.

1
Best spectacle · Queensbury

Adirondack Balloon Festival

This is the one option this weekend that can become a core childhood memory. At Floyd Bennett Memorial Airport, Saturday is scheduled around a 6:30 a.m. mass ascension of up to 80 balloons, another launch of up to 50 balloons around 5 p.m., special shapes, family activities, food and an evening Balloon Moonglow around 7:45. The easier family version is the afternoon and evening block—unless your children willingly function before dawn.

DriveAbout 2 hr 15–30 min
Best windowSaturday afternoon–Moonglow
Big variableWind and flight status

Why it earns the drive: Watching dozens of balloons inflate and launch together is fundamentally different from another fall fair. The Moonglow adds a second payoff after sunset, so the day still has a centerpiece even if the afternoon launch is delayed.

Check first: Read the official 2026 schedule and restrictions immediately before leaving. Every balloon activity is weather-permitting, and the pilots can cancel for wind even when the ground looks calm. Dogs, drones and smoking are prohibited. Allow extra traffic time and consider paid VIP parking only after comparing the general-parking plan. Our Lake George with kids guide gives you a second-day plan if you stay over.

2
Best close-to-home event · Jordan

Jordan Fall Festival — 80th-anniversary weekend

Jordan's long-running community festival has a legitimate milestone this year, and Saturday's 7:45 p.m. anniversary fireworks make it more than a standard collection of rides and food stands. The grounds also have live entertainment, crafts, merchants and the traditional fresh doughnuts and pressed cider. Sunday adds the vehicle show at 9 a.m., the pole-cross and greased-pole events, and a 3–5 p.m. hometown-music program.

DriveAbout 25–35 minutes
Saturday anchorFireworks, 7:45 p.m.
AdmissionFree festival entry

Dad plan: Arrive Saturday in late afternoon, eat before the dinner rush, give the kids a defined ride budget and stay for the fireworks. If Sunday morning rain clears, the car show and greased-pole competition provide a completely different second-day version.

Check first: Save the official festival program and daily schedule. Fireworks and outdoor entertainment remain weather-sensitive, and street closures change the easiest route into the village.

3
Best animal day · Utica

International Red Panda Day at Utica Zoo

Utica Zoo is making Saturday's visit more focused without making it complicated. Keeper chats at the red-panda exhibit are scheduled for 10:30, 11:30 and 12:30, and children can use a themed craft-and-activity table and collect a conservation trading card featuring the zoo's own red pandas, Bao and Momo. The rest of the zoo remains open for a normal full visit.

DriveAbout 55–65 minutes
Best arrivalBefore 10:30 a.m.
Admission$11 adult; $8 ages 2–12

Why it fits: The scheduled keeper talks give the day structure while the zoo supplies enough variety for both a 6-year-old and a 10-year-old. It is also far less weather-dependent than the balloon festival, even though most exhibits still involve outdoor walking.

Book/check first: Confirm the chat times on the official Utica Zoo events page and buy regular admission if you want to avoid the ticket window. The separate 9 a.m. Pilates-with-the-lions class requires its own booking and is not part of Red Panda Day.

4
Best art-and-food festival · Palmyra

The Purple Painted Lady Festival

This enormous grassroots art festival brings together 350 artists, 25 food trucks and nine bands on a farm property outside Palmyra. The scale matters: there is enough variety for children to find something interesting, while adults get a legitimate art and maker event rather than a kiddie festival with three craft booths. Live demonstrations and the Witchy Women performances at 11 a.m. and 2 p.m. help break up the walking.

DriveAbout 55–70 minutes
HoursSat. 9–5; Sun. 10–4
Admission$10; 12 & under free

Best version: Go Saturday morning while the ground and everyone's patience are fresh. Treat it as a two-to-three-hour browse plus lunch, not an obligation to inspect all 350 booths.

Check first: Review the official map, parking details and vendor list. Golf-cart shuttling is limited to specified parking areas; pets and smoking are prohibited. Sunday morning rain could make the grass and parking less pleasant.

5
Best Adirondack day trip · Inlet

Inlet Fall Festival

Fern Park hosts more than 50 vendors plus live music, food, jugglers, face painting, balloon twisting and raffles. Free admission, parking, entertainment and shuttle service keep the family math simple. The real reason to consider the two-hour drive is that the festival can anchor a complete Inlet day: Arrowhead Park, Fourth Lake views, village shops and an early-fall Adirondack atmosphere all sit close together.

DriveAbout 2 hr 5–20 min
HoursSat. 10–4; Sun. 10–3
CostFree admission and shuttle

Why it earns the drive: The festival alone is not two hours better than Jordan. Inlet plus the festival is. Go only if the family wants the Adirondacks, and build in a lake stop rather than driving up solely to browse vendors.

Check first: Use the official Inlet event page for parking and shuttle details. Saturday is currently the better weather day, near 61°F; bring layers. Our Adirondacks family guide helps if you turn it into an overnight.

6
Easy local option · Downtown Syracuse

Festa Italiana Syracuse

Downtown's Italian celebration fills the area in front of City Hall with Italian food, music, dancing, bocce, historical displays, street performers and heritage programming. For this family, the appeal is not trying to occupy an entire day—it is walking in hungry, choosing one performance or presentation, and leaving before the children experience festival saturation.

DriveAbout 5–15 minutes
HoursSat. 11–11; Sun. 12–7
Best forFood + a short outing

Why it makes the cut: It is the lowest-commitment option and still feels like an event. That matters on a weekend when Sunday's forecast may reduce everyone's appetite for a long drive.

Check first: Pick a target performance from the official entertainment and heritage schedule, then check the downtown parking and street-closure guidance. If Sunday morning rain lingers, wait for the later opening rather than standing under tents at noon.

My honest ranking for your family

  1. Adirondack Balloon Festival Saturday afternoon — the biggest memory if the launch forecast holds.
  2. Jordan Fall Festival Saturday evening — best balance of easy, inexpensive and genuinely time-limited.
  3. Utica Zoo Saturday morning — the safest bet with strong kid appeal and useful scheduled talks.
  4. Purple Painted Lady Saturday — best for art, food and browsing; less ideal if the kids hate shopping.
  5. Inlet Fall Festival Saturday — make the Adirondacks the point and the festival the anchor.
  6. Festa Italiana — excellent low-effort local food outing, not the weekend's grand adventure.

If it were my weekend, I would watch the balloon festival's wind update Friday night. If the Saturday afternoon launch looks viable, I would make that the big family play. If it looks doubtful, I would pivot immediately to Utica Zoo in the morning and Jordan's fireworks that evening—two strong plans without gambling four-plus hours of driving.
