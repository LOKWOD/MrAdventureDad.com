Central New York Weekend Guide

What to Do With the Kids: September 12–13

Six plans worth leaving the house for: two excellent local festivals, the final weekend for a Rochester favorite, vintage race cars at Watkins Glen and two Adirondack wild cards.

Use Saturday for the bigger outdoor plan

As of Wednesday morning, Syracuse and Rochester are projected to be mostly sunny and near 80°F Saturday. Sunday looks warmer and more humid around Syracuse, with more cloud cover and an isolated thunderstorm possible. Nothing here screams washout, but Saturday is the better day for Seabreeze, the race track or a long Adirondack drive. Recheck before buying weather-dependent tickets.

1
Best overall · Baldwinsville

Golden Harvest Festival at Beaver Lake

This is the rare local festival where the setting is part of the attraction. Beaver Lake fills its grounds with music and entertainment on three stages, nature games and crafts, live-animal programs, artists, food trucks, fresh doughnuts and pie-eating contests. Free canoeing, the scavenger hunt and horse-drawn hayrides make it a much better family proposition than wandering rows of vendors.

DriveAbout 25–35 minutes
WhenSat.–Sun., 10–5
Admission$5 adult; $1 ages 4–15

Dad plan: Arrive near 10, do the canoe or nature activity before the crowd peaks, eat an early lunch and save the main stage for the part of the afternoon when everybody needs to sit down. With children priced at one dollar, this is also the easiest plan to abandon gracefully if energy goes sideways.

Check first: Read the official festival page and the county event listing for parking and shuttle instructions. The festival runs rain or shine; pets are not the move in a crowd like this. Our Beaver Lake with kids guide covers the trails and family logistics.

2
Best free plan · Liverpool

Haudenosaunee Wooden Stick Festival

Onondaga Lake Park hosts a weekend built around the Creator's Game at the lake shore where the sport began. Families can watch wooden-stick lacrosse, see sticks being made, catch the Smoke Dance Competition, try Haudenosaunee foods and browse work from Native artisans. It is active, culturally meaningful and free—three things that rarely arrive in one event.

DriveAbout 10–20 minutes
WhenSat.–Sun., 10–5
AdmissionFree

Why it fits: The lacrosse gives children a live focal point while demonstrations and food keep the day from feeling like one long tournament. If you want a shorter visit, pick one match block and one cultural program rather than trying to stay all five hours.

Check first: Review the current festival details and the Upstate Lacrosse Foundation page. The associated Restore Our Sacred Lake 5K starts Saturday morning before the main festival; do not arrive expecting ordinary park access without checking the event footprint.

3
Last chance this season · Rochester

Seabreeze Final Fling Weekend

Seabreeze closes its 2026 season after Sunday, and Saturday's near-80°F forecast is close to ideal for one last rides-and-slides day. The compact park works especially well when one child is ready for bigger rides and another still wants gentler ones: families can move between the water park, classic rides, coasters and kiddie areas without spending half the day hiking across asphalt.

DriveAbout 1 hr 25–35 min
Weekend hoursRides 11–7; slides 11–6
Final Fling ticketFrom $40.99 online

Why it earns the drive: This is a true deadline, not manufactured urgency. If the kids want a park day before summer disappears, Saturday is the cleanest answer within the drive limit.

Buy/check first: Measure the kids in shoes, compare ticket categories and confirm the official hours and attraction status before paying. Weather and maintenance can close individual rides. Our amusement-park survival plan helps prevent the first-hour sprint from wrecking the afternoon.

4
Most unusual · Watkins Glen

Hilliard U.S. Vintage Grand Prix

Watkins Glen International turns into a rolling history of motorsport from Thursday through Sunday. Saturday has practice and qualifying for everything from muscle cars and Historic Trans Am to formula cars, prototypes and vintage stock cars; Sunday's schedule is dominated by sprint and feature races. The paddock is open with admission, which means children can see the machines and teams at human scale instead of watching every lap from a distant seat.

DriveAbout 1 hr 30–45 min
Best family daySaturday
Kids12 & under free with adult

Why it earns the drive: This is not a standard car show. The cars race, the categories change all day, and paddock access turns the waiting between sessions into part of the experience. Saturday's separate auto show runs 9 a.m.–3 p.m.; Sunday offers more actual racing if that matters more than variety.

Book/check first: Buy admission and save the detailed official event schedule. Bring properly fitted hearing protection for both kids—the track is loud even when vintage cars look small. See our family hearing-protection guide.

5
Adirondack museum day · Blue Mountain Lake

The Rustic Fair at Adirondack Experience

The Adirondack Experience brings craftspeople onto its campus for two days of handmade furniture, furnishings and Adirondack art. On its own, that could lean too adult. Here it works because the fair is included with admission to the entire museum campus, so families can alternate maker booths with historic boats, outdoor exhibits, regional stories and hands-on museum time.

DriveAbout 2 hr 30–45 min
WhenSat.–Sun., 10–5
Weather edgeSaturday

Why it earns the drive: It is a civilized all-day Adirondack outing for families who want something more interesting than another midway. The museum gives the children a change of pace while the fair gives adults a reason to linger.

Buy/check first: Confirm museum admission and the official Rustic Fair details. Blue Mountain Lake is currently forecast sunny and around 70°F Saturday, with a greater shower and thunderstorm risk Sunday. Pair the drive with our Adirondacks family-weekend guide only if you are staying overnight.

6
Car-family wild card · Lake George

Adirondack Nationals Car Show

Hundreds of rods, customs and classics take over the Fort William Henry grounds, with the town's famous Canada Street cruise running Friday and Saturday nights. For a child who names cars from the back seat, it is a sensory-overload home run. For a child who does not care about cars, the crowd and two-hour drive make this the wrong choice—so let the family's enthusiasm decide.

DriveAbout 2 hr 10–25 min
SaturdayShow 7–5; cruise 7–10
Admission$20; 12 & under free

Best version: Arrive late morning, see the show, take a village or lake break, then decide whether everybody has enough gas for the evening cruise. Do not commit a tired six-year-old to 15 hours just because the cars keep going.

Check first: Review the official schedule, parking information and cruise restrictions before making the drive. The $20 spectator admission covers the weekend and children 12 and under are free. Use our Lake George with kids guide to build a non-car reset into the day.

My honest ranking for your family

  1. Golden Harvest Saturday morning — the best mix of low drive, low cost and activities that actually suit both kids.
  2. Wooden Stick Festival — the most meaningful free plan and an easy half-day.
  3. Seabreeze Saturday — the strongest kid-vote road trip, with a real end-of-season deadline.
  4. Vintage Grand Prix Saturday — the unusual choice if cars and machinery light anybody up.
  5. Rustic Fair Saturday — worth the long haul when the museum is the destination and the fair is the bonus.
  6. Adirondack Nationals — excellent for a car-obsessed family, too much for everyone else.

If it were my weekend, I would do Golden Harvest Saturday at opening and keep the Wooden Stick Festival as Sunday's flexible local plan. If the kids get one big vote and want rides, skip both and make Saturday the final Seabreeze day.
