Central New York Weekend Guide

What to Do With the Kids: October 17–18

A LEGO-and-glass convention, Halloween at two remarkable places, a famous sheep festival, a pumpkin train and a chairlift day worth the mileage.

Saturday is the indoor day; Sunday is the outdoor day

As of Wednesday morning, Syracuse is forecast to see a brief morning shower, wind and a chilly high near 47°F Saturday, then mostly sunny skies near 59°F Sunday. Corning follows the same pattern, with a windy 48°F Saturday and sunny 60°F Sunday. Old Forge could struggle to reach 36°F Saturday before rebounding to about 52°F Sunday. Build Saturday around the zoo or Corning, then use Sunday for Rhinebeck, the pumpkin train or West Mountain. Recheck wind—not just rain—before any chairlift or rail plan.

1
Best overall · Corning

Empire Brick Con at Corning Museum of Glass

This is the rare event that improves an already excellent museum instead of competing with it. Hundreds of original LEGO creations fill the campus alongside collaborative build sites, games, vendors and LEGO-themed hot-glass demonstrations. The result works for brick kids, art kids and adults—and Saturday's cold, windy forecast barely matters once everyone is inside.

DriveAbout 1 hr 45 min–2 hr
DatesSaturday & Sunday
Family valueAges 17 and under free

Best version: Treat the convention as the hook, then deliberately add one regular museum anchor: a live hot-glass demo, the hands-on innovation galleries or a Make Your Own Glass project. Museum admission covers two consecutive days, although the add-on glass projects cost extra.

Book/check first: Reserve museum admission and any glassmaking slot through the official Empire Brick Con page. Limited brick-themed projects include a $32 bead for ages 10+, a $27 pressing for ages 14+ and a $54 cup for ages 14+; the museum says these fill quickly. Our Finger Lakes rainy-day plan and museum-day system will keep the visit from turning into an eight-hour gallery march.

2
Best local plan · Syracuse

Zoo Boo Plus Syracuse Stage

The Rosamond Gifford Zoo opens its daytime, “kooky-not-spooky” Halloween run with trick-or-treat stations, animal encounters, keeper chats, games, photo stops and a costume parade. Saturday is the stronger version: Syracuse Stage performs This Girl Laughs, This Girl Cries, and This Girl Does Nothing at 11 a.m. and 1 p.m. in the zoo amphitheater. The family-themed play is recommended for grades 3–6 and costs nothing beyond zoo admission.

DriveAbout 10–20 minutes
HoursSat. & Sun. 10–3
Admission$9 adult; $5 ages 3–17

Why it is the local winner: This can be a full outing without becoming an expensive production. The Halloween program is included with regular admission, members enter free and children 2 and under are free. Bring the kids in costume and bring your own treat bags.

Check first: Choose Saturday only if the stage production genuinely fits your children; otherwise Sunday should be warmer and less wind-exposed. Review any day-of changes on the official Zoo Boo page, then use our Rosamond Gifford Zoo guide to choose a short animal route instead of chasing every station.

3
Best big-drive festival · Rhinebeck

New York State Sheep & Wool Festival

This is not a generic craft fair with three sheep parked outside. Nearly 50 years of scale brings livestock and breed barns, sheep and goat shows, dog demonstrations, shearing, fiber competitions, author talks, working makers and hundreds of vendors to the Dutchess County Fairgrounds. Curious children have moving animals and demonstrations to follow while adults get one of the Northeast's deepest fiber-and-farm gatherings.

DriveAbout 2 hr 45 min–3 hr
DatesOctober 17–18
Tickets$15 presale; 11 & under free

The honest fit: Go Sunday. Rhinebeck is currently forecast sunny near 59°F, while Saturday will be cooler and windy. This is worth the mileage for an animal-loving or making-curious family; it is not worth six hours in the car merely to shop for yarn.

Book/check first: Buy the one-day ticket through the official ticket page; gate admission rises to $18. Download the current schedule and circle two animal demonstrations before driving. Parking can be slow around the fairgrounds, so arrive for the first program you truly care about rather than an arbitrary opening time.

4
Most story-worthy · Thendara

Adirondack Railroad Pumpkin Train

From Thendara Station near Old Forge, vintage coaches make a 90-minute round trip to Carter Station and Wally's pumpkin patch. Children who ride in costume receive a free pumpkin. It is short enough for younger train fans, climate-controlled if the weather turns raw and unusual enough to become the weekend's story rather than another visit to a farm.

DriveAbout 1 hr 55 min–2 hr 10 min
Departures10, noon, 2 and 4
Fare$20 adult; $15 child

Weather call: Sunday is the sensible day. Old Forge is currently forecast windy and only about 36°F Saturday, then partly sunny near 52°F Sunday. The coaches are climate-controlled, but the pumpkin-patch portion is not; dress for the actual temperature and wind.

Book/check first: The railroad's excursion page is live for the fall season, but the public page does not promise every departure on every date. Verify October 17 or 18 inventory before committing to the drive on the official Pumpkin Train page. Pair the train with lunch or a short village walk, not a second major attraction. Our Adirondacks family guide and fall layering guide cover the practical side.

5
Best for older kids · Mumford

Spirits of the Past: A Walk in the Dark

Genesee Country Village turns its historic village into a 75-minute candlelit theatrical walk built around the dark and macabre stories of Edgar Allan Poe. It is part history lesson, part outdoor theater and part seasonal atmosphere, with food, bonfires and the “Fright” House Pub before or after the tour. This is a richer evening than a standard haunted house, but the museum specifically says it is not recommended for young children.

DriveAbout 1 hr 25–35 min
This weekendFri.–Sun. 5–9
TourAbout 75 minutes outside

Best family fit: Choose this for tweens or teens who can handle uneven paths, darkness and Poe-style macabre storytelling. There are no jump scares or truly shocking scenes. Sunday's 5:30 p.m. tour is sensory-friendly with no prowlers and includes ASL interpretation.

Book/check first: Buy a timed slot online through the official Spirits page. Tickets are not sold onsite, sell out quickly and cannot be transferred to another date or time. Strollers and wagons are not permitted past the Toll House; the event runs rain or shine, so use the family weather-cutoff plan before paying for a nonrefundable slot.

6
Best active Sunday · Queensbury

West Mountain Fall Festival

West Mountain is the action-heavy alternative: scenic chairlift and hay-wagon rides, an aerial treetop park, pumpkin painting, inflatables, pony rides, a petting zoo, live music and optional mountaintop picnic packages. Admission itself is free, so a family can choose one memorable paid anchor rather than accidentally buying every activity.

DriveAbout 2 hr 15–30 min
HoursSat. & Sun. 9–2
AdmissionFree; activities extra

Why Sunday: The current Syracuse-to-Queensbury pattern favors the warmer, sunnier day, and the lift is the entire reason to drive this far. If wind shuts the chair or the family does not want the aerial park, this drops behind Zoo Boo or Corning immediately.

Book/check first: Pre-purchase only the activities you will actually use through the official festival page, then confirm chairlift and aerial-park status Sunday morning. Pets and outside coolers are not permitted. If you make the long drive, our Lake George family guide has nearby low-pressure add-ons—but keep one off-ramp in the plan.

My honest ranking for your family

  1. Empire Brick Con Saturday — the weekend's strongest mix of novelty, weather resistance and real value for families.
  2. Zoo Boo Saturday — the easy local win, especially for grades 3–6 who will enjoy the Syracuse Stage production.
  3. Sheep & Wool Sunday — the best special-event road trip if the animals and making culture genuinely excite the family.
  4. Pumpkin Train Sunday — the most memorable option for younger train kids, if inventory remains.
  5. Spirits of the Past — excellent for older children; a poor fit for preschoolers or stroller-dependent families.
  6. West Mountain Sunday — worthwhile only while the lift and outdoor activities are operating.

If it were my weekend, I would do Empire Brick Con Saturday and keep Sunday local at Zoo Boo. For one big drive, I would choose Rhinebeck Sunday for an animal-and-maker family or the Pumpkin Train Sunday for younger kids. Do not gamble on West Mountain until the wind forecast and lift status are clear.
