Central New York Weekend Guide

What to Do With the Kids: October 31–November 1

A free final day at Great Escape, Halloween aboard a vintage train, molten-glass pumpkins, two serious science days and one excellent local Sunday option.

Saturday is made for the special events; Sunday is your easy reset

As of Wednesday morning, Syracuse is forecast sunny to partly cloudy near 57°F Saturday and a pleasant 62°F Sunday. Queensbury should be mostly sunny near 57°F during Boo Fest; Corning and Rochester should reach the upper 50s with some clouds and sun. There is no rain-driven reason to hide indoors. The real constraint is the calendar: five of this guide's six special programs happen on Halloween itself, while the MOST gives families the cleanest Sunday option.

1
Best overall road trip · Queensbury

Free Boo Fest at Great Escape

Great Escape has added one final park day on Halloween, and the base event is unusually good value: free admission, free parking, trick-or-treating at the Storytown houses, a Monster Mash show, family activities and a costume contest for children 12 and under. If the kids want rides, a $9.99 wristband covers unlimited rides on a selected lineup in Timbertown, Hot Rod USA and Ghost Town.

DriveAbout 2 hr 15–30 min
HoursSaturday noon–5
Base admissionFree

Why it earns the drive: This is the park's final chance of the 2026 season, the forecast is dry, and a family can decide inside the gate whether the available rides justify the add-on. It feels like a real destination day without requiring full theme-park ticket prices.

Check first: Read the official Halloween-day announcement Friday night and again before leaving. The $9.99 ride wristband is sold inside the park and only selected rides are promised. Arrive with costumes that can safely board rides and use our Lake George with kids guide if you want an early lunch or short Queensbury-area stop before the noon opening.

2
Most distinctive · Waterloo

Finger Lakes Phantom Flyer

This one-hour Halloween train is deliberately family-friendly: vintage passenger cars, a costume contest, complimentary candy and a color-changing lemonade mocktail for young riders. The route moves through forest and open-water scenery from Waterloo, so the ride works as an experience for adults too—not just a vehicle delivering children to a candy table.

DriveAbout 50–65 minutes
DeparturesSaturday 11 & 1:30
Fare$25 adult; $19 child

Why it ranks this high: The schedule is tight enough to pair with lunch or a short Finger Lakes stop, but the vintage-car setting makes it memorable on its own. Children receive the $19 fare when riding in costume; processing fees are extra.

Book first: Check live inventory on the official Phantom Flyer page before planning anything else. Book the whole group under one reservation to sit together. Only selected historic cars are ADA accessible, so choose the listed accessible car during checkout if needed. Boarding is at 210 West Williams Street in Waterloo; parking and arrival instructions come with the confirmation.

3
Best local plan · Syracuse

EEK = MC² at the MOST

The MOST layers a full monster-themed activity menu onto the regular museum both Saturday and Sunday: DIY bats, monster constellations, pixel art, mask photos, mummification, temporary tattoos, creature building and ghost-lollipop making. Splatter Monsters starts at noon, treat stations run around the museum, and the normal hands-on exhibits remain available.

DriveAbout 5–15 minutes
DatesSaturday & Sunday
ActivitiesIncluded with admission

Why Sunday is smart: Most Halloween specials disappear after Saturday, but this program explicitly continues November 1. That makes it the low-stress answer if Saturday belongs to neighborhood trick-or-treating or a timed road trip. Build the visit around the noon Splatter Monsters activity and leave before museum fatigue takes over.

Check first: Confirm opening hours and any ExploraDome add-on on the official EEK = MC² listing. All listed Halloween activities are included with admission. Our MOST with kids guide and museum-day system help choose a two-hour route instead of trying to complete every station.

4
Best art-and-making day · Corning

Final Great Glass Pumpkin Experiment

Saturday is the last day of Corning Museum of Glass's fall program and the final Great Glass Pumpkin Experiment of the season. The Hot Glass Team turns molten glass into strange, colorful and sometimes monstrous pumpkins in the Amphitheater Hot Shop. The museum's galleries, regular demonstrations and hands-on spaces make this a complete day even if the family skips the paid Studio project.

DriveAbout 1 hr 45 min–2 hr
Special programSaturday only
WeatherDry; upper 50s

Best version: Watch the live pumpkin demonstration, give the galleries a focused route, and add Make Your Own Glass only if every participant has a confirmed reservation. The Studio projects have age minimums, cost extra and are the piece most likely to sell out.

Book/check first: Verify the demonstration time and reserve any Studio project through the official Fall Fun page. Do not promise a make-your-own pumpkin until checkout is complete. Our Corning Museum with kids guide covers the practical family route.

5
Best full science day · Rochester

Tricks & Treats at RMSC

Rochester Museum & Science Center is doing more than handing out candy. The Saturday program includes eyeball slime, squishy circuits, a blood-typing lab, kinetic-sand graveyards, Electricity Theatre, fossils and space, Rochester Ghostbusters and a giant Stay Puft figure, alongside trick-or-treat stations and the regular museum.

DriveAbout 1 hr 25–35 min
HoursSaturday 11–3
Event fee$5 + museum admission

Why it fits: Children can move between messy making, live demonstrations, familiar characters and serious exhibits, which gives mixed-age families multiple ways to reset. It is the strongest choice here for a child who wants Halloween and a parent who still wants a destination-grade museum day.

Book/check first: The $5 event ticket does not include museum admission, so price the complete visit on the official Tricks & Treats page before committing. Presenter details can change. Our RMSC with kids guide helps choose anchor exhibits and whether a separate planetarium show is worth extending the day.

6
Best free museum event · Albany

New York State Museum Halloween Spooktacular

The State Museum's Saturday program fills the fourth-floor terrace with creepy art projects, ghoulish games, spooky science specimens and dancing, ending with a children's costume parade at 1 p.m. The program is free and the museum gives the drive more substance than the event alone.

DriveAbout 2 hr 15–30 min
HoursSaturday 10–1
AdmissionFree program

The honest fit: Choose Albany over Queensbury only if the family prefers museums and state history to rides. There is no candy or snack service in the Spooktacular itself, so this is a costume-and-activities program rather than a trick-or-treat haul.

Check first: Review the official State Museum listing, then plan parking and lunch before you drive. Arrive well before the 1 p.m. parade; do not treat 1 p.m. as an arrival time. Pairing this with a carefully chosen museum gallery makes the mileage worthwhile.

My honest ranking for your family

  1. Great Escape Saturday — the best combination of novelty, good weather and unusually low cost; the ride wristband keeps the budget decision flexible.
  2. Phantom Flyer Saturday — the most distinctive limited-capacity experience, and the first thing to book if trains excite the kids.
  3. MOST Sunday — the cleanest local plan and the rare Halloween program that explicitly survives into November 1.
  4. Corning Saturday — the strongest art-and-making road trip and the final glass-pumpkin demonstration of the year.
  5. RMSC Saturday — the deepest science itinerary, but remember the $5 event fee sits on top of museum admission.
  6. State Museum Saturday — an excellent free program for museum-minded families, though Great Escape offers more payoff at nearly the same drive.

If it were my weekend, I would book the 11 a.m. Phantom Flyer Saturday if seats remain, because capacity—not weather—is the biggest failure point. Without train tickets, I would drive to Great Escape for the noon opening. Sunday is deliberately easy: sleep in, then use the MOST for a compact two- or three-hour science day.
