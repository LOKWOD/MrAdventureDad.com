Central New York Weekend Guide

What to Do With the Kids: November 7–8

The Toy Hall of Fame's new class, 19th-century winter preparations, pumpkin-smashing zoo animals, a hometown astronaut and two hands-on local programs.

Use Saturday for indoor anchors; save the outdoor-heavy trip for Sunday

As of Wednesday morning, Syracuse is forecast mostly cloudy and cooler Saturday with a shower possible, near 51°F. Sunday should remain mostly cloudy but slightly warmer near 54°F. Rochester carries a couple of Saturday showers and afternoon breeze, then a drier mix of clouds and sun Sunday near 53°F. That makes The Strong the safest Saturday road trip and Genesee Country Village the smarter Sunday choice.

1
Best overall · Rochester

National Toy Hall of Fame Celebration at The Strong

The newest National Toy Hall of Fame inductees are unveiled, then turned into a full weekend of playable experiences inside one of the region's best family museums. The special program includes a construction zone using inducted toys such as cardboard, Tinker Toys and Lincoln Logs, hands-on American Girl play for the line's 40th anniversary, and multiple kinds of slime in the Play Lab.

DriveAbout 1 hr 25–35 min
CelebrationSat. & Sun. 11–3
Event accessIncluded with admission

Why it wins: The Hall of Fame announcement makes this specific weekend different, while the rest of The Strong protects the day if one activity misses. Saturday also offers two guided LEGO workshops for ages 8 and older; Sunday adds an 11:30 a.m. preview of The Nutcracker. That gives Jacob an unusually good Saturday add-on, while Sunday is the more balanced date for both children.

Book/check first: Buy museum admission and review the official Hall of Fame celebration schedule. The optional LEGO workshop is separate, limited and costs $40 for nonmembers including all-day admission or $15 for members; choose the 10:30 a.m. or 1 p.m. session only if an age-8-plus child genuinely wants a guided build. Our Strong Museum with kids guide covers parking, pacing and paid add-ons.

2
Best Sunday road trip · Mumford

Holiday Market & Preparing for Winter

Genesee Country Village combines a real historic-village program with its holiday market. Costumed interpreters demonstrate how 19th-century families prepared for cold weather through candle dipping, holiday baking, pomander making, food preservation, winter clothing, Cat's Cradle and two-person sawing. The day also includes Indigenous storytelling and collaborative Haudenosaunee cooking with seasonal ingredients.

DriveAbout 1 hr 25–40 min
HoursSat. & Sun. 10–3
Online tickets$15 adult; $13 youth

Why it earns the drive: Children have jobs to watch and try while adults have history, foodways and a large market of regional makers. It is much more substantial than a generic holiday shopping event. Sunday gets the drier regional forecast and avoids forcing an outdoor village walk through Saturday's shower risk.

Book/check first: Buy in advance through the official event page; door prices are one dollar higher. Ages 4–12 are the youth tier and children 3 and under are free. Bring a Thanksgiving-themed nonperishable if you want to support the museum's pantry collection, and check which food locations will operate before leaving.

3
Best local animal day · Syracuse

Squishing of the Squash at Rosamond Gifford Zoo

The zoo reuses its Zoo Boo pumpkins as animal enrichment, with fifteen scheduled feedings and play sessions from 10:30 a.m. through 3 p.m. The route moves from siamangs and lemurs to camels, tiger, elephants, red panda, Andean bear, Komodo dragon, otters, sloth and armadillo. This is not one ceremonial pumpkin toss—it can structure most of a zoo visit.

DriveAbout 10–20 minutes
DateSaturday
ProgramIncluded with admission

Best family route: Arrive before the 12:15 tiger session, continue to the elephants at 12:30, red panda at 1 and Andean bear at 1:15, then move indoors for Komodo dragon, porcupine and meerkat if the shower arrives. That compact middle block delivers the strongest animals without asking Lily to chase every stop from opening.

Check first: Screenshot the official animal schedule because times are subject to change. Dress for a cool shower and use our zoo-with-kids route to connect the outdoor and indoor buildings efficiently.

4
Best free learning day · Syracuse

CNY Veterans Parade & Expo

The morning expo inside the Center of Progress includes military and veterans exhibits, historical artifacts and a children's area. This year's standout is Syracuse-born NASA astronaut Jeanette Epps speaking at 10 a.m. about the path from Corcoran High School and Le Moyne College to nearly eight months aboard the International Space Station. A military K-9 program follows at 11, the parade begins at noon and the 10th Mountain Division Band performs at 1.

DriveAbout 10–20 minutes
ExpoSaturday 9–2
CostFree admission & parking

Why it fits: The astronaut talk gives children a concrete science-and-service anchor before the vehicles, marching bands and more than 90 military and veterans organizations in the hour-long parade. Most of the morning is indoors, which matters with Saturday's shower risk.

Check first: Confirm the sequence on the official event site and arrive early enough to be seated before the 10 a.m. talk. The parade is outside around Chevy Court, so pack a compact rain layer and decide before noon whether the weather is worth the full loop.

5
Best small-group program · Marcellus

Family Field Days: Pioneer Crafts

Baltimore Woods turns found and reused materials into a two-hour family craft session inspired by pre-screen entertainment. Families work on traditional projects such as dolls, candles and other practical handmade objects, then can add a short trail walk if the shower window cooperates.

DriveAbout 20–30 minutes
TimeSaturday 1–3
Family feeUp to $15; members free

Why it makes the cut: This is guided, hands-on time at a real nature center rather than an open craft table. The limited group size should make it calmer than the museum and fairgrounds choices, and the indoor/outdoor mix works for a changeable November afternoon.

Book first: Online family registration is required and space is limited. Use the official program page before building the rest of Saturday. Our Baltimore Woods with kids guide helps choose a short trail extension without overloading the afternoon.

6
Best free creative stop · Auburn

Schweinfurth Clay & Sewing Studios Open House

The Schweinfurth Art Center opens its newly refurbished Clay Studio and Sewing Studio for a free, family-friendly afternoon. Visitors can try hands-on creative activities, see the weekly quilt-making community, tour the workspaces and enjoy music and light refreshments.

DriveAbout 35–45 minutes
HoursSaturday noon–3
AdmissionFree

The honest fit: This is a focused three-hour open house, not an all-day destination. It is strongest as a low-cost creative afternoon or paired with lunch and one carefully chosen Auburn stop—not as the sole reason for a rushed full-day itinerary.

Check first: Verify the activity list through the current CNY Arts listing or call the center before leaving; hands-on supplies can be capacity-limited even when admission is free. If you want to extend the day, our Seward House with kids guide offers an age-fit check before adding another museum.

My honest ranking for your family

  1. The Strong Saturday — the most weather-proof, genuinely special day; the LEGO workshop is unusually well matched to Jacob if a seat remains.
  2. Genesee Country Village Sunday — the richest outdoor/history option and the right date for the drier forecast.
  3. Squishing of the Squash — the best local kid-first plan, especially the tiger-to-bear sequence from 12:15 to 1:15.
  4. Veterans Parade & Expo — extraordinary free value because the astronaut talk, K-9 demonstration, parade and 10th Mountain band form a real program.
  5. Baltimore Woods — the strongest smaller, calmer activity, but registration capacity decides whether it is viable.
  6. Schweinfurth — a worthwhile free creative afternoon, provided you treat it as a focused Auburn outing rather than a major road trip.

If it were my weekend, I would make The Strong the Saturday anchor and skip the separate LEGO workshop unless Jacob specifically wants the miniature arcade build. Sunday, I would choose Genesee Country Village only if everyone wants a second full day out; otherwise keep it local and easy. The best no-ticket Saturday is Jeanette Epps at the Veterans Expo at 10, the parade at noon, then home before the day turns into work.
