Central New York Weekend Guide

What to Do With the Kids: October 3–4

A living-history agricultural fair, an Adirondack day built specifically for kids, free local fall fun and three strong interest-specific road trips.

Use Saturday for the long outdoor drive

As of Wednesday morning, Syracuse is trending mostly sunny Saturday near 65°F and milder but cloudier Sunday near 68°F. Mumford and the central Adirondacks follow the same basic pattern: Saturday looks a little brighter, while Sunday stays comfortable. That makes Saturday the better gamble for Inlet or Fort Ticonderoga; Sunday works well for the Genesee Country Village fair, Adirondack Experience or a close-to-home plan. Recheck mountain forecasts and event operating notices Friday night.

1
Best overall · Mumford

Genesee Country Village Agricultural Fair

This is the rare fall fair where the demonstrations are the reason to go. Across the historic village, families can find livestock and milking demonstrations, sheep herding, a poultry show, working horses, apple pressing, Haudenosaunee corn traditions, cornhusk weaving, falconry and blue-ribbon competitions. Punch & Judy marionettes and the “Tent of Wonders” give younger children obvious anchors; the 19th-century village keeps it substantial for adults.

DriveAbout 1 hr 25–35 min
HoursSat. & Sun. 10–4
Advance tickets$24 adult; $18 ages 4–12

Why it wins: It is a full museum day with a strong event layered through it, not a vendor fair that expects the children to entertain themselves. Sunday adds vintage base ball at 11 a.m. and 1 p.m.; Saturday gets the slightly brighter current forecast.

Book/check first: Buy through the official Agricultural Fair page to save $1 per ticket, then screenshot the tickets—the museum warns that Wi-Fi can be spotty at the entrance. Parking is free and plentiful; the event is rain or shine. Use our family museum-day system to keep the village from becoming an endurance march.

2
Best kid-specific road trip · Inlet

Adirondack Kids Day

Inlet builds a compact village-and-park day around things children can actually do: French Louie's Fishing Derby, live raptors, magic, geocaching, a bounce house, authors, crafts, face painting and balloon twisting. Fishing poles, life jackets and worms are provided, which removes the usual gear barrier. Arrowhead Park and Fourth Lake give the trip an Adirondack setting instead of a parking-lot festival feel.

DriveAbout 2 hr 5–20 min
WhenSaturday 10–3
CostFree

Why it is worth the drive: The day is designed around children rather than merely admitting them. Saturday's current Inlet forecast is around 61°F and mostly sunny—cool enough for layers, but excellent for walking and fishing.

Check first: Start at the Kiwanis booth for the day's schedule, a goody bag while supplies last and fishing-derby signup. Verify any final changes on the official Inlet event page, and leave Syracuse early enough to arrive before 10. Our first fishing trip and no-cell-service plan are useful prep.

3
Best local plan · North Syracuse

Chuck Hafner's Fall Festival Opening Weekend

Hafner's opening weekend combines the everyday fall setup—a 400-square-foot corn pit, straw maze, petting zoo, mini golf, giant games, teepee and tricycle speedway—with unusually good free wildlife programming. Saturday has a creepy-crawly reptile, amphibian and invertebrate program from 1 to 2:30 p.m. Sunday has live birds-of-prey shows at 11:30 a.m., 12:30 p.m. and 1:30 p.m. A pottery market runs both days.

DriveAbout 10–20 minutes
Festival windowWeekend activities 11–4
Weather backupUndercover; rain or shine

Best version: Choose Sunday if raptors will land better than reptiles with the kids; choose Saturday for the longer pottery market. Keep it a focused two- or three-hour outing. The core attractions and wildlife programs are free, while bounce zones, face painting, zombie paintball, tie-dye and carnival games use paid tickets.

Check first: Confirm the wildlife program and ticketed-activity pricing on the official Fall Festival page. Set an optional-activity budget before arrival; the free corn pit, maze, animals and presentation are already a complete outing. Pair it with our Syracuse family day-trip guide if you want lunch or a second short stop.

4
Big-drive history pick · Ticonderoga

Fort Ticonderoga Heritage, Harvest & Horse Festival

For a history-minded family, this is the most ambitious day on the list: working-horse and ox demonstrations, horse-drawn wagon rides, historic hands-on programs, a farmers' market, the King’s Garden and the six-acre Heroic Corn Maze, all layered onto the fort's regular tours, trades and demonstrations. The corn maze's new 2026 design follows six Revolutionary War stories rather than treating history as decoration.

DriveAbout 3 hr–3 hr 15 min
WhenSaturday; grounds 9:30–5
AdmissionFestival included with daily ticket

The honest catch: This sits just beyond the three-hour line from parts of Syracuse, so it only earns the gas if the fort, animals and corn maze all appeal. Do not make the trip for a generic fall fair; make it for a full Fort Ticonderoga day that happens to have an excellent fall festival inside it.

Book/check first: Buy admission and review the official festival details. The optional 75-minute Carillon boat cruise needs a separate reservation and can sell out. Check the route Friday night and use our road-trip packing system before committing to six-plus hours in the car.

5
Best Sunday stretch trip · Blue Mountain Lake

FallFest at Adirondack Experience

FallFest adds apple pressing, autumn crafts, fiber arts, demonstrations, music, activities and games to an already substantial open-air museum campus. More than 20 buildings across 120 acres, a climbable fire tower and the family-oriented exhibits give the children things to do even if one craft station misses. This is a museum destination first and a seasonal event second—that is why it can justify the drive.

DriveAbout 2 hr 35–50 min
WhenSunday 10–5
Online admission$22 adult; $13 ages 5–17

Who should choose it: Families wanting an Adirondack road trip with a reliable destination at the end, not a quick festival browse. The museum recommends at least four hours, so this needs an early start and a deliberate lunch plan.

Book/check first: Purchase discounted online admission and confirm the program on the official FallFest page. FallFest is included with admission. The museum campus is sloped, the log hotel is closed for restoration and the boat-borrowing program has closed for the season; do not promise those pieces.

6
Best machinery day · NYS Fairgrounds

Big East Powersports Show

This is an interest-specific pick, but a strong one for children who light up around motorcycles, ATVs and snowmobiles. Saturday includes a vintage snowmobile show, ATV demonstrations, a scavenger hunt, inflatable castle and obstacle course, plus Globe of Death motorcycle shows at 11 a.m., 1:30 p.m. and 5:30 p.m. It is indoors at the Expo Center, so it also serves as the weather-proof option.

DriveAbout 10–20 minutes
WhenSaturday 9–7
Current checkout$13 adult; 13 & under free

Who should choose it: A machine-obsessed family. Everyone else will get more variety at Genesee Country Village or Hafner's. The Globe of Death can be loud and intense, so bring properly fitted hearing protection and be willing to step out.

Book/check first: Check the official Saturday schedule and Expo Center information. The ticket checkout also lists a $20 two-adult family package with children 13 and under included. Use our family hearing-protection guide before the stunt show.

My honest ranking for your family

  1. Genesee Country Village Saturday at opening — the richest all-ages day and the least dependent on one headline activity.
  2. Adirondack Kids Day Saturday — the memorable road trip if fishing, raptors and village-scale fun get an immediate yes from the kids.
  3. Hafner's Sunday for the birds-of-prey shows — the best low-effort local choice.
  4. Fort Ticonderoga Saturday — exceptional for a history-and-horses family, but only worth six-plus hours of driving when everyone is committed.
  5. ADKX FallFest Sunday — a strong destination museum with seasonal programming, not a quick festival.
  6. Big East Saturday — excellent for machine kids; easy to skip otherwise.

If it were my weekend, I would do Genesee Country Village at 10 a.m. Saturday and Hafner's birds-of-prey program Sunday. If the children are excited by the fishing derby and live raptors, swap GCV for Inlet—that is the more unusual family story, and Saturday's current Adirondack forecast is cooperating.
